## What reviewers want to know

A motivation letter answers three questions: **who you are, why this program, and what you will do after it**. Reviewers read hundreds of letters, so make your answers clear and specific.

## A structure that works

1. **Opening (3 to 4 lines)**: a real moment or problem that pushed you toward your field. No quotes from famous people.
2. **Your background**: your studies, projects, work or volunteering. Pick two or three facts that prove you are ready.
3. **Why this program and country**: name courses, professors, labs or the skills you will gain. Show you read the program page.
4. **Your plan after graduation**: what you will do, where, and how it helps your community or country.
5. **Closing**: one or two lines that connect everything.

## Common mistakes

- Repeating your CV line by line.
- Writing the same letter for every scholarship.
- Very long sentences and general words like "passionate" without examples.
- Going over the word limit. Respect it exactly.

## Before you submit

Read it out loud, ask a teacher or friend to check it, and make sure the scholarship and university names are correct.
