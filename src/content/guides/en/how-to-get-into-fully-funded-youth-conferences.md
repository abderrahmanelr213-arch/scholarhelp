## Short answer

**Organisers of fully funded youth conferences pick people who show one real action and a clear reason to attend.** Read the theme, choose one experience that fits it, tell it with numbers and results, and explain what you will do with what you learn. Apply early, follow every instruction, and never pay anyone to "guarantee" a seat.

## What does "fully funded" cover at a youth conference?

It usually means the organiser pays your return flight, hotel and meals during the event, and the programme itself. Some also pay visa fees or a small allowance. Read the exact list on the official page, because "fully funded" sometimes means only a few seats. On ScholarHelp, the Quick overview of each listing shows what is covered and any fee.

## How do organisers choose participants?

Most selection teams look for the same four things:

1. **Fit with the theme.** A climate forum wants people who work on climate; a leadership camp wants people who already lead something, even something small.
2. **Real action.** What did you actually do? A tutoring group with 30 students, a fundraiser, a research poster, a small business.
3. **Clear motivation.** Why this event, and why now? What will you take home?
4. **Diversity.** They want a mix of countries, backgrounds and fields, so being from a less represented country can help you.

## What should I write in each part of the application?

**"Tell us about yourself."** Two or three sentences: who you are, what you study or do, and the one cause you care about most. Skip your birthplace and hobbies unless they matter.

**"Describe a project or leadership experience."** Use this order: the problem, what you did, the result (with a number), and what you learned. Example: "Our village school had no library, so I collected 400 books with two friends and set up a reading corner that 60 children now use every week."

**"Why do you want to attend?"** Name something specific on the programme: a session, a speaker, a skill. Then say what you will do after: share it with your club, start a project, train others.

**Recommendation.** Ask a teacher or supervisor who knows your work. Give them at least two weeks, the deadline and a short list of what you did together.

## What mistakes get applications rejected?

- Sending the same general essay to every programme.
- Big words with no example ("I am passionate about changing the world").
- Ignoring word limits or missing documents.
- Applying on the last day, when the website is slow.
- Choosing events that do not fit your field or age.

## How do I avoid paid "fully funded" summits?

Check the official website, look for any application or participation fee, and count how many seats are really funded. If you are asked to pay to secure a place, stop. Our guide on [fake scholarships and paid summits](/guides/fake-scholarships-and-paid-summits/) has a 5-minute check.

## What should I do after I am accepted?

Reply before the confirmation deadline, start your visa application the same week, and keep every email from the organiser. Many send an invitation letter for the embassy. Plan to share what you learn when you return, because organisers of future events will ask what you did with it.
