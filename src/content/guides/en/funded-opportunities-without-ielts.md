## Short answer

**Yes, you can win a funded opportunity without IELTS.** Leadership programmes, youth forums, science conferences and some research grants often ask for no test score at all. They judge your English from your essay, video, interview or reference letter. For full degree scholarships, the university usually sets the rule, and many accept a letter showing your last degree was taught in English.

## Why do some opportunities not ask for IELTS?

- **Short programmes and conferences** last days or weeks. The organisers only need you to follow talks and speak with others, so they look at how you write and speak in your application.
- **Your referee can confirm your level.** Some programmes send your professor a short form that includes a question about your English.
- **Research grants** often let your host supervisor decide whether your English is enough for the project.
- **Degree scholarships** follow the university's rules, and many universities waive the test if your previous degree was taught in English.

## How do I prove my English without IELTS?

- **Medium of Instruction (MOI) letter.** Ask your university for an official letter saying your degree was taught in English.
- **A previous degree in English.** If you studied in English, many universities count that as proof.
- **An interview.** Some programmes test your English in a short online call.
- **A cheaper online test.** Some universities accept the Duolingo English Test, which you can take at home.
- **Your application itself.** A clear, well-written essay and a confident video are the proof for most conferences and forums.

## How to check the language rule in 2 minutes

1. Open the **official** page of the opportunity, not a copy on another site.
2. Look for a section called "Language requirements", "English proficiency" or "Eligibility".
3. Search the page for the words **IELTS**, **TOEFL**, **waiver** and **Medium of Instruction**.
4. If it is still unclear, send a short, polite email to the contact address and ask.

On ScholarHelp, every opportunity page shows the language requirement in a yellow box under the summary, so you can see it at a glance.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Trusting old "no IELTS" lists.** Rules change every year. Check the current call.
- **Paying "agents" to skip IELTS.** No real scholarship needs a middleman. Apply yourself on the official site.
- **Ignoring the host university.** A scholarship may set no test while the university you join still asks for one.
