## What "fully funded" really means

A fully funded scholarship usually covers **tuition, a monthly living allowance and travel**. Some also cover health insurance, visa fees and a language course. Always read the "benefits" section: a "full tuition" scholarship is not the same as fully funded.

## Where the best scholarships come from

- **Governments**: Chevening (UK), DAAD (Germany), Türkiye Burslari, MEXT (Japan), GKS (Korea), Stipendium Hungaricum.
- **Groups of universities**: Erasmus Mundus joint master's programs in Europe.
- **Universities themselves**: many give merit scholarships to their best international applicants.
- **Foundations and companies**: often for specific fields, regions or women in science.

## Check eligibility before anything else

Open the official call and look for four things:

1. **Nationality**: is your country on the list?
2. **Level and age**: bachelor, master or PhD, and any age limit.
3. **Grades and language**: minimum GPA, IELTS or TOEFL, or a language waiver.
4. **Work experience**: some programs, like Chevening, require two years.

If you fail one rule, move on. There are many other opportunities.

## Plan your year

Most big scholarships open on a yearly cycle. Many close between **October and March** for studies that start the next September. Start preparing your documents three to six months before the deadline.

## Avoid scams

A real scholarship **never asks you to pay** to receive it. Apply only on the official website. ScholarHelp always links to the official page.
