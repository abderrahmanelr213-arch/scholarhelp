## Short answer

**Yes, you can study a master's in Europe with a full scholarship, whatever your nationality.** The strongest options are Erasmus Mundus joint master's, government scholarships such as France's Eiffel programme, and university scholarships such as ETH Zurich's Excellence Scholarship. Most deadlines for the 2027 intake fall between November 2026 and January 2027, so start now.

## What types of full scholarships are there?

- **Erasmus Mundus joint master's.** You study in two or more European countries, and the scholarship covers participation costs, insurance and a monthly allowance. Read [how to apply for Erasmus Mundus](/guides/how-to-apply-for-erasmus-mundus-scholarships/).
- **Government scholarships.** A country pays international students to study there, often through the university.
- **University scholarships.** A single university waives tuition and pays a stipend to its best applicants.

## Which ones are open now for 2027?

These are on ScholarHelp with their official links, funding details and language rules:

- [ETH Zurich Excellence Scholarship](/opportunities/eth-zurich-excellence-scholarship-2027/), Switzerland. A living stipend and full tuition waiver. Apply in November 2026.
- [Eiffel Excellence Scholarship](/opportunities/eiffel-excellence-scholarship-2027/), France. A monthly allowance for master's and PhD students. You apply through a French university, which sends files by 6 January 2027.
- [EMLE Erasmus Mundus Master in Law and Economics](/opportunities/erasmus-mundus-emle-law-economics-2027/). Non-European applicants apply by 31 December 2026.
- [EUROPUBHEALTH+ Master in Public Health](/opportunities/europubhealth-erasmus-mundus-public-health-2027/). Up to €1,400 a month, fees and insurance covered. Apply by 8 January 2027.
- [Erasmus Mundus CLMCE in Children's Literature](/opportunities/erasmus-mundus-clmce-childrens-literature-2027/), for students of literature, education and media.

Deadlines change every year, so always confirm the date on the official page before you start.

## What language do you need?

Most master's programmes in Europe are taught in English and ask you to prove it. Typical rules are IELTS Academic 6.5 to 7.0 or TOEFL iBT 90 to 100, but many accept a bachelor's degree taught fully in English instead. A few French or German-taught programmes ask for that language. If tests are a problem, read [Duolingo vs IELTS vs TOEFL](/guides/duolingo-vs-ielts-vs-toefl-for-scholarships/) and [funded opportunities without IELTS](/guides/funded-opportunities-without-ielts/).

## How should you plan your applications?

1. **Pick 3 to 6 programmes** that match your degree and goals.
2. **Make a deadline table** with each programme's date, documents and language rule.
3. **Prepare your core documents once**: CV, motivation letter, transcripts and two recommendation letters. Use our [documents checklist](/guides/scholarship-application-documents-checklist/).
4. **Adapt your motivation letter** for each programme. A letter that names the course, its partner universities and your plan after graduation is much stronger. See [how to write a motivation letter](/guides/how-to-write-a-motivation-letter/).
5. **Submit early.** Portals get slow on the last day.

## Is it really free?

Fully funded means the main costs are covered, but check the details. Some scholarships pay tuition directly and give you a monthly amount for everything else, so you still need to plan for your flight, visa and the first weeks before the first payment. Our guide on [whether a stipend is enough](/guides/scholarship-stipend-is-it-enough/) shows how to check.
