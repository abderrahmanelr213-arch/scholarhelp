## Short answer

**If your degree was taught fully in English, you may not need IELTS or TOEFL.** Many programmes accept an official letter from your university, called a medium of instruction (MOI) letter or English proficiency certificate, instead. It only works when the official page says so, and the letter must be clear, signed and stamped.

## When does an MOI letter work?

It usually works when the official page says something like "not required if your previous degree was taught in English". Examples on ScholarHelp:

- the [ETH Zurich Excellence Scholarship](/opportunities/eth-zurich-excellence-scholarship-2027/): no test needed if your bachelor's was taught fully in English
- the [Yenching Academy](/opportunities/yenching-academy-2027-peking-university/): graduates of English-taught degrees are exempt

It usually does not work when the page asks for a specific score with no exceptions, or exempts only people from certain countries. For example, the [EMLE Erasmus Mundus master's](/opportunities/erasmus-mundus-emle-law-economics-2027/) exempts degrees from listed English-speaking countries only.

## What must the letter say?

A good MOI letter includes:

- your full name as in your passport
- your degree, field and graduation date
- the years you studied
- a clear sentence: "The entire programme was taught and assessed in English."
- the official letterhead, stamp and signature of the registrar, dean or head of department
- the date, and contact details for verification

Some universities also want it sent directly by email from your university. Ask before you request it.

## How do you get one?

1. **Ask your faculty or registrar office.** Many universities have a standard form.
2. **Bring proof** such as your transcript and student ID.
3. **Ask for several signed copies** and a scanned PDF.
4. **Check the wording** before you leave the office. A letter that says "some courses were in English" will not be accepted.

Add it to your [scholarship documents checklist](/guides/scholarship-application-documents-checklist/) early, because offices can be slow.
