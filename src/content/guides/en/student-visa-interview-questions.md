## Short answer

**A student visa interview checks three things: that you are a real student, that your studies are paid for, and that your plans after studying are clear.** Answer honestly, briefly and in your own words. Know your programme, your university and your funding well, bring every document on the embassy's list, and stay calm.

## What do visa officers want to know?

Almost every question fits into one of these groups:

- **Your studies:** what you will study, and why this programme.
- **Your university:** why you chose it and what you know about it.
- **Your money:** who pays for tuition, living costs and travel.
- **Your plans:** what you will do after you finish.

If you prepare a clear answer for each group, you are ready for most interviews.

## What are the most common questions?

1. **Why do you want to study in this country?** Talk about the programme and what it gives you, not only about the country.
2. **Why this university or programme?** Name one or two real things: a course, a lab, a professor or the programme structure.
3. **What will you study?** Explain your field in one or two simple sentences.
4. **Who is paying for your studies?** Say what your scholarship covers and how you will pay for anything it does not cover.
5. **Have you studied this subject before?** Link your previous degree or work to the new programme.
6. **What will you do after you finish?** Describe a realistic plan. Many embassies want to see how your studies connect to your future work.
7. **Do you have family in this country?** Answer truthfully. A simple yes or no with a short explanation is enough.

## How should I answer?

- **Be honest.** Never invent facts or give answers you memorised from someone else.
- **Be short and clear.** Answer the question, then stop. Officers often have little time.
- **Be specific.** "I will study public health at [University] for two years" is better than "I want to study abroad".
- **Be consistent.** Your answers should match your application, your study plan and your documents.
- **Stay calm.** If you do not understand a question, ask politely for it to be repeated.

## What documents should I bring?

Use the embassy's official list first. Most embassies ask for:

- your passport and visa appointment confirmation
- your admission or invitation letter
- your official scholarship or funding letter, showing exactly what is covered
- proof of any extra funds, if the scholarship does not cover everything
- your diplomas, transcripts and, if needed, language test results

Our [student visa checklist](/guides/student-visa-checklist/) explains the documents in more detail. Organise them in one folder, in the same order as the embassy's list.

## What mistakes should I avoid?

- **Not knowing your own programme.** Read your admission letter and programme page again before the interview.
- **Unclear funding.** Know the exact amounts in your scholarship letter.
- **Answers that do not match your documents.** Check your study plan and motivation letter before you go.
- **Arriving late or without copies.** Plan your route and bring copies of important papers.

## How do I prepare?

Practise your answers out loud with a friend, in the interview language.
