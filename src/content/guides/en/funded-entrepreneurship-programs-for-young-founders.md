## Short answer

**Funded entrepreneurship programmes pay founders to build their idea, usually with a grant, training, mentors and sometimes travel.** The best ones are equity-free and charge no fee. To get in, show a real problem, proof that people want your solution, a clear plan for the money, and a team that can deliver. Most programmes do not ask for IELTS.

## What types of funded programmes exist?

- **Government startup grants and accelerators:** public agencies give equity-free money to attract founders. Some ask you to move to the country during the programme.
- **Private accelerators:** a few months of training and investor access, often with an investment in exchange for a small share of your company.
- **Social entrepreneur fellowships:** stipends, training and networks for people solving social or environmental problems.
- **Youth competitions and summits:** you send an idea or essay, and finalists get a funded trip and sometimes prize money. One example is the [St. Gallen Symposium Global Ideas Competition](/opportunities/st-gallen-symposium-global-ideas-competition-2027/).

## What does "equity-free" mean, and why does it matter?

Equity is ownership of your company. An equity-free grant gives you money without taking any share. That is the best deal for an early founder. Some grants ask you to add your own money too (co-funding), for example 10% to 50% of the budget, so read the funding rules carefully.

## What do selection panels look for?

- **a real problem:** who has it, how often, and what it costs them
- **traction:** users, pilots, letters of interest, sales, or even interviews with customers
- **the team:** why you are the right people, and the skills you already have
- **a clear plan:** what you will build or test with the money, and how you will measure progress
- **fit:** why this programme, this country or this network helps you now

## How do you write a strong application?

1. **Start with one sentence** that explains what you do and for whom.
2. **Use numbers.** "40 farmers tested our app in 2 months" beats "great feedback".
3. **Be honest about risks** and how you will test them.
4. **Keep the pitch video short and simple.** Show your face, your product and your team.
5. **Ask someone outside your field to read it.** If they cannot explain your idea back to you, rewrite it.

## How do you avoid paid "programmes" that look funded?

Read the official page for words like "participation fee", "registration fee" or "self-funded seats". Real government grants and well-known accelerators rarely charge to apply.
