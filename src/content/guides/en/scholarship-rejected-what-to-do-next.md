## Short answer

**A rejection is normal and rarely means you are not good enough. Most funded scholarships reject far more strong applicants than they accept.** Give yourself a day, ask politely for feedback if the programme allows it, look honestly at your documents, and apply again with a stronger application. Your CV, transcripts and most of your letter can be reused for the next deadline.

## What does a rejection usually mean?

There are only a few common reasons:

- **Strong competition:** there were more excellent applicants than places.
- **A missing or wrong document:** one missing file can stop an application before anyone reads it.
- **Not meeting a requirement:** such as the degree level, age limit, test score or nationality.
- **A weak fit:** the motivation letter did not show why this programme, and why you.
- **Weak references:** short or generic recommendation letters.

## Should I ask for feedback?

Yes, if you do it politely and briefly. Send one short email to the programme office:

```
Subject: Request for feedback – [Your name], [Scholarship name] 2027

Dear [Programme] team,

Thank you for considering my application. I respect your decision.
If possible, I would be grateful for any short feedback that could
help me improve a future application.

Kind regards,
[Full name]
```

Many large programmes cannot answer every applicant, so do not send reminders or argue with the decision.

## How do I make my next application stronger?

1. **Check every requirement first.** Read the eligibility rules again and only apply where you clearly qualify.
2. **Rewrite your motivation letter.** Make it specific to the programme, with clear goals and real examples. See our guide on [how to write a motivation letter](/guides/how-to-write-a-motivation-letter/).
3. **Improve your CV.** Add new experience, projects or volunteering, and use numbers to show results. Our [scholarship CV guide](/guides/how-to-write-a-cv-for-a-scholarship/) has a simple template.
4. **Choose better referees.** Pick people who know your work well and give them time. Read our guide to [recommendation letters](/guides/recommendation-letters/).
5. **Apply early and to several programmes.** Do not depend on one scholarship. Use the full [documents checklist](/guides/scholarship-application-documents-checklist/) to avoid missing files.

## Can I apply to the same programme again?

Usually yes. Many programmes welcome reapplicants, and some ask what has changed since your last application. Show progress: a new job, a project, better grades or a clearer plan. Check the official rules, because a few programmes limit how many times you can apply.
