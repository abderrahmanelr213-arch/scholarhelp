## Short answer

**To get an Erasmus Mundus scholarship, choose one or more programmes from the official catalogue, check each one's entry rules, and apply directly on the programme's own website before its scholarship deadline, usually between October and January.** There is no separate scholarship form: when you apply to the programme, you are considered for the scholarship. Strong applications match the programme closely, have good grades and clear references, and meet the language rule.

## What is an Erasmus Mundus scholarship?

An Erasmus Mundus Joint Master is a master's degree run by several universities together. You study in at least two European countries, and you graduate with a joint or multiple degree. The European Union funds full scholarships for the best-ranked applicants from anywhere in the world.

Under the [EU programme rules](https://erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu/programme-guide/part-b/key-action-2/erasmus-mundus), the scholarship:

- covers the whole programme (usually 12, 18 or 24 months);
- pays a living allowance of €1,400 a month;
- covers tuition and participation costs, and contributes to travel, visa and installation costs.

No more than 10% of a programme's scholarships can go to students of one nationality, so applicants from every country have a real chance.

## How do I apply, step by step?

1. **Pick programmes that fit your degree.** Read the entry rules first: the bachelor's field, the minimum grades, the language test and any work experience. Do not apply to a programme you do not clearly qualify for.
2. **Note the scholarship deadline.** Many programmes have an early date for scholarship applicants and a later one for self-funded students. Only the early date counts for the scholarship.
3. **Prepare your documents.** You usually need your degree and transcripts, a CV (often Europass), a motivation letter, two recommendation letters, a language certificate and a passport copy. Some programmes ask for a research idea or a portfolio.
4. **Write a motivation letter for each programme.** Name the programme's courses, universities or mobility tracks that you want, and say how they fit your past work and your plans. Our guide on [how to write a motivation letter](/guides/how-to-write-a-motivation-letter/) has a simple structure.
5. **Ask for references early.** Give your referees at least two weeks, the deadline and a short list of what you did with them. See our guide to [recommendation letters](/guides/recommendation-letters/).
6. **Submit on the programme's own portal.** Upload clear PDFs, check file names and size limits, and keep the confirmation email.
7. **Wait for results.** Most programmes announce scholarship results in spring. If you are on the reserve list, reply quickly when asked.

## How do I make my application stand out?

- **Match the programme, not Europe in general.** Selection committees want to know why this master, these universities and this track.
- **Show results.** A project, an internship or a thesis with a clear outcome says more than a list of interests.
- **Keep your grades and documents clean.** Certified translations, a clear transcript and a correct CV avoid early rejection.
- **Meet the language rule fully.** If a programme asks for IELTS 6.5 with no part below 6.0, a 6.5 overall with a 5.5 part may fail the check.
- **Plan the visa early.** If you are selected, start your student visa as soon as you get the admission letter. Our [student visa checklist](/guides/student-visa-checklist/) helps.

## What mistakes should I avoid?

- Paying an agent for a "guaranteed" Erasmus Mundus place. You apply directly to the programme, and no one can guarantee a scholarship.
- Copying the same motivation letter for every programme.
- Missing the early scholarship deadline and applying in the self-funded round by mistake.
- Applying when you have already held an Erasmus Mundus master's scholarship, which is not allowed.
