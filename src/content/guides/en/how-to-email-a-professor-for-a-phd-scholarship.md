## Short answer

**Find two to three professors whose recent work matches your idea, then send each a short, personal email: who you are, why their research, what you want to study and which scholarship you are applying for.** Attach a one-page CV and a short research idea. Start two to three months before the deadline, follow up once after about a week, and move on politely if there is no answer.

## Why do I need to contact a professor?

Many funded research opportunities ask for a host or supervisor in advance. For example, the **[Swiss Government Excellence Scholarships](/opportunities/swiss-government-excellence-scholarships-2027/)** need a letter of support from a Swiss professor, and the **[Ernst Mach Grant](/opportunities/ernst-mach-grant-worldwide-austria-2027/)** needs a signed confirmation from an Austrian supervisor. Without that letter, you cannot apply.

Other programmes, such as the **[OIST PhD in Japan](/opportunities/oist-phd-program-2027-japan/)**, admit you to a graduate school first and help you choose a lab later. Even then, a polite email to a professor can strengthen your application.

## How do I find the right professor?

- Read the university's department pages and look for research groups in your field.
- Check each professor's recent papers (the last two or three years) on Google Scholar.
- Look for signs they take students: "open positions", "join us" or current PhD students in their lab.
- Choose professors whose work truly matches your idea, not just famous names.

## What should my first email say?

Keep it short and specific:

1. **Subject line:** clear and useful, for example "Prospective PhD student: [your topic], [scholarship name] 2027".
2. **Who you are:** your degree, university and main result (one or two sentences).
3. **Why them:** mention one of their recent papers and how it connects to your interest.
4. **What you want:** your research idea in two or three sentences.
5. **The scholarship:** name it and its deadline, and say what you need from them (a support letter or confirmation).
6. **Attachments:** your CV and a one-page research idea.

## A simple email template

```
Subject: Prospective PhD researcher: [topic] – [Scholarship name] 2027

Dear Professor [Last name],

My name is [Full name]. I recently completed a [degree] in [field]
at [university], where my thesis looked at [topic, one line].

I read your [year] paper on [topic] with great interest. I would
like to build on it by studying [your idea in one or two sentences].

I am planning to apply for the [scholarship name], which requires a
letter of support from a host professor by [deadline]. Would you be
open to discussing whether this project could fit in your group?

I have attached my CV and a one-page outline of my idea.
Thank you for your time.

Kind regards,
[Full name]
[Email] · [Country]
```

## What mistakes should I avoid?

- Sending one email to many professors at once, or starting with "Dear Sir/Madam".
- Writing a long life story instead of a clear research idea.
- Getting their name, title or research area wrong.
- Asking "do you have any scholarship for me?" without naming a programme.
- Following up every day. One polite reminder is enough.

## What do I do after a professor says yes?

Thank them, agree on the next steps and send them what they need early: the programme's support-letter form, your research proposal and the deadline. Then finish your application documents.
