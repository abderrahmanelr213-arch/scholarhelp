## Short answer

**A real scholarship rarely asks you to pay to apply.** Before you apply, check three things: is it on the organiser's **official website**, does it clearly say **what is paid and for how many people**, and does it ask for **money or bank details**? If the official site is missing, or you are asked to pay to "secure" a place, stop.

## Is a "fully funded" summit really free?

Not always. Many conferences run by the UN, universities, foundations and governments cost nothing to apply. Some private youth summits work differently:

- They charge every applicant a fee, often 10 to 20 US dollars.
- Only a few seats are "fully funded". Most people get a "partially funded" or "self-funded" offer and must pay for flights or a participation fee.
- The words "fully funded" are in the title, but the small print shows the real numbers.

These events can still be useful, but go in with clear eyes. Read how many fully funded seats exist, what a partial offer covers, and what you would pay in total.

## How do I check an opportunity in 5 minutes?

1. **Find the official website.** Search the organisation's name, not the opportunity title. The call must be on its own domain (for example a university or UN site), not only on social media.
2. **Read "What is covered".** Look for the exact list: flights, housing, meals, visa, stipend. Vague words like "funding support" or "airfare support" can mean only part of the cost.
3. **Look for a fee.** Search the page for "fee", "payment" and "registration". Note the amount and whether it is refundable.
4. **Count the funded places.** "Up to 10 fully funded seats" for thousands of applicants is very different from a programme that pays for everyone selected.
5. **Check the contact.** Real organisers use their own email domain. Be careful with free email addresses and WhatsApp-only contact.

## What are the red flags of a scholarship scam?

- You are asked to pay to "secure", "reserve" or "process" your scholarship.
- You "won" something you never applied for.
- They ask for your bank card, bank login or a money transfer.
- There is no official website, or the site was created very recently.
- They push you to answer within hours.
- An "agent" promises a guaranteed scholarship for a fee.

## Should I ever pay?

Sometimes paying is normal: a university admission fee, a visa fee, a language test or a document translation. These go to the university, embassy or test centre, never to a person on social media. If a fee is not explained on the official site, do not pay it.
