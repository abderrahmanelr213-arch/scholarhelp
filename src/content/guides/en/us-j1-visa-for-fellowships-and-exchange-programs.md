  - q: What is a J-1 visa?
    a: The J-1 is a US exchange visitor visa. It is used for exchange programmes, fellowships, research stays, internships and some short training programmes. You get it through a sponsor approved by the US Department of State, which is usually the organisation running your programme.
  - q: What is the DS-2019 form?
    a: The DS-2019 is the document your sponsor issues after you are accepted. It shows your programme, dates and funding. You need it to pay the SEVIS fee, fill in the DS-160 form and book your visa interview.
  - q: Who pays for the J-1 visa?
    a: It depends on the programme. Some fully funded fellowships pay or refund the visa and SEVIS fees, and some government-sponsored programmes are exempt from them. Others expect you to pay. Check your offer letter or ask the programme before you pay anything.

## Short answer

**If you win a funded fellowship or exchange programme in the USA, you will most likely travel on a J-1 exchange visitor visa.** The programme (your sponsor) sends you a DS-2019 form. You then pay the SEVIS fee, fill in the online DS-160 form, pay the visa fee if your programme does not cover it, and attend an interview at a US embassy. Start as soon as you get your DS-2019, because interview waits can be long.

## Which programmes use the J-1 visa?

Many of the funded opportunities we share do, including:

- leadership and youth fellowships, such as the [Hansen Leadership Institute](/opportunities/hansen-leadership-institute-2027-usa/)
- government exchange programmes and summer institutes
- research stays and visiting scholar programmes
- some paid internships and training programmes

Your programme will tell you which visa to apply for. Do not apply for a tourist visa to join a fellowship.

## What are the steps?

1. **Get accepted and receive your DS-2019.** Check that your name, dates and funding are correct.
2. **Pay the SEVIS I-901 fee** online, unless your programme pays it or you are exempt. Keep the receipt.
3. **Fill in the DS-160** online visa application and print the confirmation page.
4. **Pay the visa application fee** and book an interview at the US embassy or consulate in your country.
5. **Go to the interview** with your documents.
6. **Travel.** You can usually enter the US up to 30 days before your programme start date.

Fees change, so always check the current amounts on the official US embassy website for your country.

## What documents should you bring to the interview?

- a valid passport
- your DS-2019, signed
- the DS-160 confirmation page
- the visa fee and SEVIS fee receipts
- a recent photo, if your embassy asks for one
- your acceptance letter and proof of funding
- proof of ties to your home country, such as studies, a job or family

Our [student visa checklist](/guides/student-visa-checklist/) covers these documents in more detail.

## What will they ask at the interview?

The officer wants to know three things: that your programme is real, that it is funded, and that you will go home afterwards. Expect questions such as "What will you do in the programme?", "Who is paying?" and "What will you do when you return?" Answer briefly and honestly. Our guide to [visa interview questions](/guides/student-visa-interview-questions/) has sample answers.

## What should you watch out for?

- **The two-year home residency rule.** Some J-1 participants must spend two years at home before applying for certain US visas. Check your DS-2019.
- **Hidden costs.** Even fully funded programmes may not pay for your passport, visa fee or travel to your home airport. Read the funding section carefully. See [fully funded vs partially funded](/guides/fully-funded-vs-partially-funded-scholarships/).
- **Refusals.** If you are refused, ask the programme for advice before reapplying. A refusal is not always final.
