## Short answer

**A good scholarship CV is one or two pages, newest items first, and shows proof of what the committee wants: strong studies, real experience, leadership and a clear direction.** Use simple headings, short lines that start with a verb, and numbers where you can. Leave out photos and personal details, and make sure every fact matches your motivation letter and documents.

## What does a scholarship committee look for in a CV?

Reviewers often read hundreds of applications, so they scan your CV in less than a minute. They look for:

- **Academic strength:** your degree, grades and any academic awards.
- **Experience:** research, internships, jobs or volunteering related to your field.
- **Leadership and impact:** clubs, projects or community work where you made something happen.
- **Fit:** a clear link between what you have done and what the programme offers.

## What sections should I include?

1. **Name and contact:** full name (as in your passport), email, phone, city and country. Add a LinkedIn link if it is up to date.
2. **Education:** university, degree, dates and your grade (GPA or class). Add a thesis title if it fits the programme.
3. **Research or work experience:** role, organisation, dates and two or three lines on what you did and achieved.
4. **Projects:** academic, personal or community projects with a clear result.
5. **Leadership and volunteering:** what you led or organised, and for how many people.
6. **Awards and scholarships:** prizes, rankings or past grants.
7. **Skills and languages:** software, lab or technical skills, and your languages with your level or test score.

Publications, conferences and certificates can have their own section if you have them.

## How do I write strong bullet points?

Start each line with an action verb and show a result. Compare:

- Weak: "Responsible for the student club."
- Strong: "Led a 30-member student club and organised 6 career workshops for 400 students."

Use numbers (people, money, percentages, rankings) whenever they are true. Keep each line to one or two rows.

## What should I leave out?

- A photo, age, marital status or religion, unless the programme asks for them
- Long paragraphs and "objective" statements
- Secondary school details, if you already have a university degree
- Skills everyone has, such as "Microsoft Word" or "hard-working"
- Anything you cannot prove or explain in an interview

## A simple one-page template

```
FULL NAME
Email · Phone · City, Country · LinkedIn

EDUCATION
BSc in [Field], [University], [Country]          2022 – 2026
GPA 3.7/4.0 (top 10%). Thesis: [title]

EXPERIENCE
Research Assistant, [Lab or Professor], [University]     2025 – now
- [Action verb + what you did + result]
Intern, [Organisation], [City]                           Summer 2024
- [Action verb + what you did + result]

LEADERSHIP AND VOLUNTEERING
President, [Club]                                        2023 – 2025
- [What you led and the result, with numbers]

AWARDS
[Award name], [Organisation]                             2024

SKILLS AND LANGUAGES
Skills: [Python, SPSS, lab techniques...]
Languages: Arabic (native), English (IELTS 7.0), French (B1)
```

Write your CV in the language of the programme, usually English.

## How do I check my CV before sending it?

- Save it as a PDF named "Firstname_Lastname_CV.pdf".
- Check that dates, names and titles match your transcripts and motivation letter.
- Read it on a phone screen to make sure it is easy to scan.
- Ask a teacher or friend to spot typos.
- Adapt the order for each programme, putting the most relevant section first.
