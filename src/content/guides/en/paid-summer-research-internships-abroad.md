## Short answer

**Paid summer research internships let undergraduates join a university lab abroad for 6 to 12 weeks, often with a stipend, free housing and travel support.** Most open in the autumn and close between December and February. To win one, choose programmes that match your field, write a focused motivation letter that names the research you want to do, and ask for a strong letter from a teacher who knows your work.

## How do these programmes work?

You apply to a programme run by a university, a department or a research institute. If you are selected, you are matched with a research group and a supervisor. You spend the summer working on a real project, and many programmes end with a short presentation or poster. The stipend and housing are meant to cover your stay, so you can focus on the work.

## What do they usually cover?

It varies, so read the official page carefully:

- **a stipend** for living costs, often paid in one or two parts
- **free or cheap housing**, such as student dorms
- **travel**, either paid in full against receipts or as a fixed allowance
- **visa costs**, in some programmes

If only part of the cost is covered, the programme is partially funded. Our guide to [fully funded vs partially funded](/guides/fully-funded-vs-partially-funded-scholarships/) explains the difference, and [is the stipend enough?](/guides/scholarship-stipend-is-it-enough/) shows how to check your real costs.

## Who can apply?

Most programmes are for bachelor's students who have finished at least one or two years, and some also accept master's students. Many are limited to natural sciences, engineering, computer science or life sciences. Check:

- your year of study and expected graduation date
- your field of study
- the minimum grade, if one is given
- the language rule: a test score, an interview, or nothing

## When should you apply?

Plan backwards from the deadline. A good timeline is:

1. **October to November:** make a list of programmes and read their pages.
2. **November:** ask for recommendation letters. See [recommendation letters](/guides/recommendation-letters/).
3. **November to December:** write your motivation letter and update your CV. See [how to write a CV for a scholarship](/guides/how-to-write-a-cv-for-a-scholarship/).
4. **December to February:** submit, well before the deadline.

## How do you make your application stand out?

- **Name the research.** Mention one or two research groups or topics on the programme's website and say why they interest you.
- **Show what you have already done.** Coursework, a class project, a lab course or coding work all count.
- **Keep the letter focused.** One page that answers "why this lab, why now, why me" is better than a long story. See [how to write a motivation letter](/guides/how-to-write-a-motivation-letter/).
- **Contact a professor only if the programme allows it.** Some ask you to choose a lab yourself. Our guide on [how to email a professor](/guides/how-to-email-a-professor-for-a-phd-scholarship/) shows how to write a short, polite email.

## Where can you find them?

Look at university science and engineering faculty pages, national research institutes and trusted opportunity sites that link to the official page. Browse the funded opportunities on ScholarHelp, where every listing links to the official announcement and says exactly what is covered.
