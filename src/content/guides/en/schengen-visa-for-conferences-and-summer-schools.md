## Short answer

**For a conference, forum or summer school of up to 90 days in Europe, you usually need a short-stay Schengen visa (type C).** Apply at the embassy or visa centre of the country where the event takes place, as soon as you have your acceptance letter. Bring the invitation, proof of what is funded, travel medical insurance of at least €30,000, and proof that you will return home.

## Which visa do you need?

Most funded events on ScholarHelp in Europe take place in Schengen countries, for example the [Heidelberg Laureate Forum](/opportunities/heidelberg-laureate-forum-2027/) in Germany, the [St. Gallen Symposium](/opportunities/st-gallen-symposium-global-ideas-competition-2027/) in Switzerland and the [Slovakia summer school grants](/opportunities/slovakia-national-scholarship-programme-summer-2027/).

- **Up to 90 days:** a short-stay Schengen visa (type C). One visa covers all Schengen countries.
- **More than 90 days:** a national long-stay visa (type D) from the country you will stay in.
- **The UK and Ireland** are not in Schengen and have their own visas.

Some nationalities do not need a visa for short stays. Check the official visa page of the country you are going to.

## Where and when should you apply?

Apply at the embassy, consulate or official visa centre of the **country where the event takes place**. If you visit several countries, apply to the one where you will spend the most days.

You can apply **up to 6 months before you travel** and **no later than 15 days before**. A decision usually takes about 15 days but can take up to 45. Book your appointment the day you are accepted.

## Which documents do you need?

- a valid passport, issued in the last 10 years and valid at least 3 months after you leave Europe
- the visa application form and recent passport photos
- the **official invitation or acceptance letter**, with the event dates
- a **letter showing what is funded** (travel, housing, meals, stipend)
- **travel medical insurance** covering at least €30,000 for the whole trip
- flight booking and accommodation details (often in the funding letter)
- proof you will return: a student certificate, a work letter, or family ties
- bank statements or a sponsor letter for any costs that are not covered

## How much does it cost?

The standard fee is **€90** for adults. The fee can be waived for students and postgraduate students travelling for study or training, for researchers travelling for scientific research, and for young people aged 25 or under going to seminars, conferences or cultural events run by non-profit organisations. Ask the organiser for a letter that confirms your role. Visa centres may charge their own service fee.

## How can you avoid a refusal?

- **Apply early.** Late applications are the most common reason people miss funded events.
- **Make the purpose clear.** Your invitation letter should name you, the event and the dates.
- **Match your documents.** Dates on your insurance, flights and letter should be the same.
- **Show you will return.** Bring proof of studies, work or family at home.
- **Ask the organiser for help.** Many funded programmes send visa support letters or contact the embassy for participants.

If you are refused, you get a letter with the reason. Fix that point and apply again, or appeal if the letter explains how.
