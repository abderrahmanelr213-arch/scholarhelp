## Short answer

**Yes, you can still apply in two weeks if you start today.** On day one, read the official page, list every document, and ask for recommendation letters. Write your essays while you wait. Finish a few days early so you have time to fix mistakes and upload calmly.

## What is a realistic 14-day plan?

**Days 1–2: plan and ask**
- Read the whole official page and write a checklist of every document.
- Email your recommenders today with your CV, the programme link and the deadline.
- Request official transcripts or certificates that take time.

**Days 3–7: write**
- Draft your motivation letter or personal statement.
- Update your CV to match the programme.
- Write short answers to any extra questions.

**Days 8–10: improve**
- Ask a teacher or friend to read your essay.
- Cut long sentences and add specific examples.
- Check that every claim in your essay also appears in your CV.

**Days 11–12: collect and check**
- Gather scans of your passport, diplomas and test scores.
- Rename files clearly, for example "Surname_CV.pdf".
- Gently remind your recommenders.

**Days 13–14: submit**
- Fill in the online form carefully and save often.
- Submit at least one day before the deadline, and keep the confirmation email.

## What can you skip when time is short?

- Do not start a new language test unless it is required and results arrive in time. Check if your old score is still valid, or if a [medium of instruction letter](/guides/english-proficiency-letter-instead-of-ielts/) is accepted.
- Do not write a new essay for every question if one strong story can be adapted.
- Do not wait for a perfect draft. A clear, complete application sent on time is the goal.

## Which mistakes happen most when people rush?

- uploading the wrong file or an old version of the essay
- forgetting the deadline's time zone
- leaving the name of another scholarship in the letter
- missing a required document because the checklist was skipped
