## Short answer

**A stipend is enough when it covers rent, food, transport, insurance and small extras in your study city, with a little left over.** Before you accept, compare the monthly amount with real local prices, list the costs the stipend does not include, such as your flight, visa, deposit and first weeks, and plan how you will pay them. Many students are surprised in their first month, not their tenth.

## What is a stipend, exactly?

A stipend is a regular payment for living costs. It is different from:

- **tuition**, which pays the university and often never passes through your hands
- **a travel grant**, which pays your flight once
- **a salary**, which comes with a work contract, as in many PhD positions. See our guide to [paid PhD positions](/guides/paid-phd-positions-how-phd-funding-works/).

A "fully funded" scholarship usually includes tuition and a stipend. Our guide to [fully funded vs partially funded scholarships](/guides/fully-funded-vs-partially-funded-scholarships/) explains the difference.

## How do I check if the stipend covers my costs?

Make a simple monthly budget for your study city:

1. **Rent:** check student residences on the university website. This is usually the biggest cost.
2. **Food:** groceries and some meals at the student canteen.
3. **Transport:** many cities have cheap student passes.
4. **Health insurance:** compulsory in many countries, sometimes paid by the scholarship.
5. **Phone, internet and small extras.**

Subtract the total from the stipend. If little or nothing is left, plan before you travel.

## What costs are often not covered?

- the **flight** to the country, unless travel is included
- the **visa fee** and documents. Our [student visa checklist](/guides/student-visa-checklist/) lists what you need
- the **rental deposit** and first month's rent, often due before the first payment
- **books, a laptop** and study materials
- **family costs**, if a spouse or children come with you

The first stipend payment can arrive several weeks after you land. Bring enough savings for at least your first month.

## What can I do if the stipend is not enough?

- **Ask the university** about cheaper housing, hardship funds and fee waivers.
- **Look for small extra grants**, for example for travel or research costs.
- **Check work rules** for your scholarship and visa before taking a part-time job.
- **Choose a cheaper city** next time: the same stipend goes much further in some cities than others.
