## Short answer

**African students can find fully funded scholarships through three main routes: programmes created for Africans, global scholarships open to every country, and government scholarships offered by individual countries.** Start about a year ahead, check the eligible countries and language rules on each official page, prepare strong documents early, and never pay to apply.

## Where can African students find fully funded scholarships?

- **Programmes made for Africa.** Some foundations fund young Africans to study at partner universities in Africa and abroad, often with leadership training and a commitment to give back.
- **Global scholarships open to every country.** Many top universities run fully funded scholarships open to students from anywhere, and some give priority to applicants from low- and middle-income countries. Examples on ScholarHelp include the **[Gates Cambridge Scholarship](/opportunities/gates-cambridge-scholarship-2027/)** and the **[Clarendon Scholarships](/opportunities/clarendon-scholarships-oxford-2027/)**.
- **Government scholarships.** Countries such as France, Switzerland and others fund international students. See the **[Eiffel Excellence Scholarship](/opportunities/eiffel-excellence-scholarship-2027/)** and the **[ETH Zurich Excellence Scholarship](/opportunities/eth-zurich-excellence-scholarship-2027/)**.
- **Erasmus Mundus master's programmes.** These joint master's degrees offer full scholarships and are open to students worldwide. Our guide on [how to apply for Erasmus Mundus](/guides/how-to-apply-for-erasmus-mundus-scholarships/) explains the process.
- **Short programmes.** Funded conferences, summer schools and exchanges are a good first step. See [how to find fully funded summer schools](/guides/how-to-find-fully-funded-summer-schools/).

## What do these scholarships usually cover?

A fully funded scholarship normally pays tuition and a living allowance, and often accommodation, insurance and a return flight. Some only pay part of these costs, so read our guide on [fully funded vs partially funded scholarships](/guides/fully-funded-vs-partially-funded-scholarships/) and check whether the stipend is enough for your city.

## What do committees look for?

- **Strong grades** and a clear academic record
- **Leadership and service**, such as community projects, clubs or volunteering
- **A clear plan to make an impact**, often in your home country or region
- **Financial need**, for some programmes
- **Good English**, shown through a test or your previous studies

Show real examples. Our guide on [how to write a motivation letter](/guides/how-to-write-a-motivation-letter/) helps you turn your story into a strong application.

## Do I need IELTS?

Not always. Many universities accept a letter showing your degree was taught in English, and some accept Duolingo or TOEFL. Read our comparison of [Duolingo vs IELTS vs TOEFL](/guides/duolingo-vs-ielts-vs-toefl-for-scholarships/) and our list of [funded opportunities without IELTS](/guides/funded-opportunities-without-ielts/).

## How do I avoid scams?

Scammers often target African applicants with fake offers. Never pay an "application" or "processing" fee to an agent, check that the organiser is real, and apply only on the official website. Read our guide to [fake scholarships and paid summits](/guides/fake-scholarships-and-paid-summits/).
