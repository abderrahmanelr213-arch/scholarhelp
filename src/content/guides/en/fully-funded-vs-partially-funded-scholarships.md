## Short answer

**A fully funded scholarship pays almost all of your costs: fees, living or accommodation, and usually travel. A partially funded one pays only some of them, and you cover the rest.** The label alone is not enough, because "fully funded" does not mean the same list everywhere. Always read the official page and check each cost item before you apply.

## What does "fully funded" usually include?

Most fully funded scholarships and programmes cover:

1. **Tuition or programme fees.** The full fee, or a complete fee waiver.
2. **Living costs.** A monthly stipend for degrees, or free accommodation and meals for short programmes.
3. **Travel.** A return flight in economy class, sometimes only up to a fixed amount.
4. **Visa and insurance.** Often the visa fee and basic health insurance, but not always.

For example, the **[Gates Cambridge Scholarship](/opportunities/gates-cambridge-scholarship-2027/)** covers the full cost of study for students from outside the UK, and the **[Hansen Leadership Institute](/opportunities/hansen-leadership-institute-2027-usa/)** is a fully funded leadership programme in the USA.

## What does "partially funded" usually mean?

A partially funded opportunity pays only part of the cost. Common examples:

- **Tuition only:** fees are waived but you pay for rent, food and travel.
- **Stay only:** accommodation and meals are covered at the event, but you buy your own flight.
- **Travel only:** a travel grant or a fixed amount towards your flight.
- **A fixed amount:** a grant of a set sum that may not cover everything.

The **[Falling Walls Female Science Talents](/opportunities/falling-walls-female-science-talents-2027/)** programme, for example, pays for trips to Berlin, while the **[Ernst Mach Grant](/opportunities/ernst-mach-grant-worldwide-austria-2027/)** gives a monthly grant for a research stay in Austria.

## Which costs are often left out?

Even "fully funded" offers sometimes leave out:

- the visa fee and travel to the embassy;
- the flight from your home country, when the programme only pays local travel;
- health insurance or a medical check;
- costs before you arrive, such as English tests, translations and document fees;
- extra costs for family members.

If the official page does not name a cost, assume it is not covered and ask the organisers by email.

## How do I check what a programme really pays?

1. **Open the official page,** not a social media repost.
2. **Find the funding section** (often called "benefits", "what we cover" or "financial support").
3. **Tick each item:** fees, stipend, accommodation, meals, flight, visa, insurance.
4. **Check limits,** such as "up to €500 for travel" or "economy class only".
5. **Check the fee.** A real programme states any application fee clearly. A large payment to "confirm your place" is a warning sign; read our guide to [fake scholarships and paid summits](/guides/fake-scholarships-and-paid-summits/).
