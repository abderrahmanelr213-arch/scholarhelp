## Short answer

**Choose the test your programme accepts. IELTS and TOEFL are accepted almost everywhere. The Duolingo English Test is cheaper and taken from home, but fewer programmes accept it.** Before you pay for any test, open the official page of each scholarship and university on your list and check which tests and minimum scores they accept. Some opportunities do not need a test at all.

## What is the difference between Duolingo, IELTS and TOEFL?

- **IELTS:** taken at a test centre (on paper or on a computer). Scores go from 0 to 9 in "bands". It is very common for the UK, Europe, Australia and Canada.
- **TOEFL iBT:** taken at a test centre or at home. Scores go from 0 to 120. It is very common for the United States and accepted widely elsewhere.
- **Duolingo English Test (DET):** taken online at home, with a short test time and fast results. Scores go from 10 to 160. Accepted by many universities, especially in the United States, but less often for government scholarships.

## Which test do scholarships accept?

Large government scholarships and many European and British universities usually ask for **IELTS or TOEFL**. Some accept the **Duolingo English Test** too, especially since online testing became common. A few programmes accept other tests, such as Cambridge English or PTE Academic.

The only safe rule: **read the official page**. Look for the words "English language requirements", "IELTS", "TOEFL", "Duolingo" or "waiver".

## Which test is cheapest and fastest?

The Duolingo English Test is usually the cheapest and fastest option. You take it from home and results usually arrive within a few days. IELTS and TOEFL cost more and the price changes by country, but they open more doors. If money is tight, first check whether all your programmes accept Duolingo before choosing it.

## Do I always need an English test?

No. Many funded opportunities do not ask for one:

- **Fellowships, summer schools and youth conferences** often only ask for "good English" and check it in the interview.
- **Some universities accept a Medium of Instruction (MOI) letter** if your previous degree was taught in English.
- **Native speakers or graduates from English-speaking countries** are often exempt.

Read our guide to [funded opportunities without IELTS](/guides/funded-opportunities-without-ielts/) for more options.

## How do I choose the right test?

1. **List your programmes.** Write down every scholarship and university you plan to apply to.
2. **Check accepted tests and minimum scores** on each official page.
3. **Pick the test most of them accept.** If one important programme only accepts IELTS or TOEFL, take that one.
4. **Book early.** Leave time for a second attempt before your deadline.
5. **Practise with free official materials** from the test organisers before you pay.
