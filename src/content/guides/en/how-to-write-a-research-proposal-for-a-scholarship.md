## Short answer

**A strong research proposal answers four questions in plain words: what problem you will study, why it matters now, how you will study it, and why you are the right person to do it.** Keep it short, be specific, show you know the recent work in your field, and give a realistic plan with steps and a timeline.

## What is a research proposal, and who asks for one?

It is a short plan for the research you want to do with the scholarship or fellowship. Funded PhDs, postdoc fellowships, visiting researcher programmes and some master's by research ask for one. Reviewers use it to judge your thinking, not to hold you to every detail.

## What structure should I use?

Use these six parts. Short headings make it easy for a busy reviewer to follow.

1. **Title.** One line that names the topic and the place or group you study. "Mobile clinics and maternal health in rural Kunar, Afghanistan" is better than "A study of health".
2. **Background and problem (about 20%).** What is known, and what is missing? End with one clear research question.
3. **Why it matters (about 10%).** Who benefits if you answer the question: patients, farmers, policy makers, other researchers?
4. **Methods (about 35%).** How you will collect and analyse data: interviews, lab work, surveys, models, archives. Say how many, where and why this method fits.
5. **Timeline and resources (about 15%).** A simple month-by-month or year-by-year plan, and what you need from the host (lab, data, supervisor).
6. **Your fit (about 20%).** Your skills, past projects and why this host and supervisor are the right match.

Add a short reference list of five to ten recent, relevant sources.

## What do reviewers look for?

- **A clear gap.** Show that you read recent work and found something not yet answered.
- **A question you can finish.** Big topics with small budgets and short timelines worry reviewers.
- **Methods that match the question.** Explain why you chose them, not just what they are.
- **Fit with the host.** Mention the lab, group or supervisor's work by name.
- **Impact.** Especially for fellowships linked to development, health or climate, say how your results will be used.

## What mistakes weaken a proposal?

- Writing a literature review with no question at the end.
- Promising to "solve" a huge problem in one year.
- Using the same proposal for every programme without changing the fit section.
- Long sentences full of jargon that a reviewer from a nearby field cannot follow.
- Ignoring the page or word limit, or the required headings.

## How do I make it stronger before I send it?

Read it aloud. Ask a teacher or researcher in your field to read it, and ask someone outside your field if they understand your question. Cut every sentence that does not help answer the four core questions. Then check the programme's rules one more time: format, length, language and file name.
