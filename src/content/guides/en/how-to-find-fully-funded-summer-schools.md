## Short answer

**A fully funded summer school pays most or all of your costs for a short programme abroad, often tuition, housing and sometimes travel or a stipend.** Start searching in autumn, because many deadlines for next summer fall between December and February. Look on official university and research-institute websites, check exactly what is covered, and prefer programmes that do not ask you to pay to apply.

## What does a funded summer school cover?

It changes from one programme to another. A summer school may cover:

- **tuition**, so you do not pay to attend
- **accommodation**, often in student housing
- **meals or a daily allowance**
- **travel**, sometimes fully and sometimes up to a fixed amount
- **a stipend**, especially in research summer schools and lab internships

"Fully funded" should mean that your main costs are paid. "Partially funded" means you still pay something, often your flight. Always read the official list of what is covered.

## When should I apply?

Plan one season ahead:

- **October to December:** research programmes and find open calls.
- **December to February:** most deadlines for July and August programmes.
- **March to May:** results, visas and travel planning.

Our list of [funded scholarship deadlines in November and December](/guides/funded-scholarship-deadlines-november-december-2026/) is a good place to start.

## Where can I find real funded summer schools?

- **University websites:** search "summer school" plus your field on the sites of universities you like.
- **Research institutes:** many science institutes run paid summer research programmes for bachelor's and master's students.
- **Government scholarship agencies:** some countries fund summer language and culture programmes for international students.
- **International organisations and foundations:** some fund short courses, forums and academies.
- **ScholarHelp:** we check every listing on the official page. Short funded programmes on the site include the **[Heidelberg Laureate Forum](/opportunities/heidelberg-laureate-forum-2027/)** and the **[Slovakia National Scholarship exchange stays](/opportunities/slovakia-national-scholarship-programme-summer-2027/)**.

## How do I get accepted?

1. **Choose programmes that fit your studies.** A clear link between the topic and your field makes your application stronger.
2. **Write a short, specific motivation letter.** Explain what you want to learn and how it helps your next step. See [how to write a motivation letter](/guides/how-to-write-a-motivation-letter/).
3. **Prepare a one-page academic CV.** Our [scholarship CV guide](/guides/how-to-write-a-cv-for-a-scholarship/) has a template.
4. **Ask for a recommendation early.** Give your professor at least two weeks.
5. **Apply to several programmes.** Places are limited, so do not depend on one.

## How do I avoid fake "fully funded" programmes?

Be careful when a programme asks you to pay before selection, sells "self-funded" seats next to a few "funded" ones, or does not name a real organiser. Read our guide to [fake scholarships and paid summits](/guides/fake-scholarships-and-paid-summits/) before you pay anything.
