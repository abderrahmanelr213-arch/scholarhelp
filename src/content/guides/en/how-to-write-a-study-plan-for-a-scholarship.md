## Short answer

**A study plan explains what you will study, why, how you will spend your time in the programme, and what you will do after graduating.** Keep it clear and realistic, about one to two pages unless the official page says otherwise. Use a simple structure: your background, your goals, the programme, a timeline, and your plans after graduation.

## Which scholarships ask for a study plan?

Study plans are common for government scholarships in Asia, such as scholarships in **China**, **Korea** and **Japan**, and for many university scholarships at bachelor's and master's level. Some programmes call it a "study plan", others a "plan of study" or "statement of study". Always read the official page. It tells you the exact name, the length, and whether it must be written in English or another language.

## How is a study plan different from other documents?

- **Motivation letter:** why you, and why this scholarship. See our guide on [how to write a motivation letter](/guides/how-to-write-a-motivation-letter/).
- **Research proposal:** one research question, your method and expected results, for PhD and research degrees. See [how to write a research proposal](/guides/how-to-write-a-research-proposal-for-a-scholarship/).
- **Study plan:** what you will study and how, with a timeline from your first semester to graduation.

## What should a study plan include?

Use these five parts. Short headings make it easy to read.

1. **Background (one paragraph).** Your current studies or work and the one or two experiences that led you to this subject.
2. **Study goals.** What you want to learn and which skills you want to gain. Be specific. "I want to learn health data analysis" is better than "I want to improve myself".
3. **Why this programme and this university.** Name real courses, labs, professors or partner organisations. This shows that you read their website.
4. **Timeline.** What you will do in each semester or year: language course, core courses, internship, final project or thesis.
5. **After graduation.** How you will use what you learn, ideally in your home country or region, and how it helps your community.

## Simple study plan template

```
Study Plan – [Full name]
Programme: [Degree], [University], [Country]

1. Background
I studied [subject] at [university]. During [project/job], I saw
[problem], which made me want to study [field].

2. Study goals
In this programme I want to learn [skill 1], [skill 2] and [skill 3].

3. Why this programme
The courses [course 1] and [course 2], and the work of
[professor/lab], match my goal of [goal].

4. Timeline
Year 1, semester 1: [language course / core courses]
Year 1, semester 2: [courses + start of project]
Year 2: [internship, thesis or final project]

5. After graduation
I plan to work as [role] at [type of organisation] in [country],
where I will use [skill] to [result].
```

## What mistakes should I avoid?

- **Copying a template word for word.** Committees read many plans and notice copied text.
- **Being too general.** Name courses, skills and a real timeline.
- **Unrealistic plans.** Do not promise to finish a two-year degree in one year.
- **Ignoring the rules.** Check the length, language and file format on the official page.
- **Forgetting the language requirement.** If you need a Chinese, Korean or Japanese language year first, include it in your timeline.
