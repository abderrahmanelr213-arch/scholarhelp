## Short answer

**In many countries a PhD is paid. Funded PhD students usually get free tuition plus a monthly salary or stipend.** In much of continental Europe, PhD students are employees with a work contract. In the UK, most funded students get a tax-free stipend. In the US, students usually get a tuition waiver and a stipend, often linked to teaching or research work. Always check what the funding covers on the official page.

## Is a PhD a job or a degree?

It depends on the country:

- **Employee model (common in the Netherlands, Germany, Austria, Switzerland and the Nordic countries):** you sign a work contract, receive a salary and usually get social security, holidays and pension contributions.
- **Stipend model (common in the UK and many scholarships):** you are a student and receive a grant for living costs. Tuition is paid separately by the funder.
- **Assistantship model (common in the US and Canada):** you get a tuition waiver and a stipend, often in return for some teaching or research assistance.

## What is the difference between a salary and a stipend?

A **salary** comes with a contract. It is taxed like normal work but also gives you social benefits. A **stipend** is a grant for living costs. It may be tax-free, but it usually comes without employment rights. Neither is "better": compare the amount you will actually receive each month with the cost of living in that city.

## Where are paid PhD positions common?

- **Europe:** many universities and research institutes advertise PhD positions as jobs, with a salary and a fixed contract, often three or four years.
- **United Kingdom:** doctoral training programmes and research councils fund tuition and a tax-free stipend, usually for three to four years.
- **United States and Canada:** most research PhDs in science and engineering are funded through fellowships, teaching or research assistantships.
- **Asia and the Middle East:** several research universities and institutes offer full funding with a monthly allowance, housing support and travel.

## What does a fully funded PhD usually include?

- tuition fees, or no tuition at all
- a monthly salary or stipend
- sometimes health insurance, visa costs and a flight at the start
- sometimes money for conferences and research

What it may **not** include: application fees, family costs, or travel home. Read the official funding page and write down exactly what is covered.

## How do I find and apply for a paid PhD?

1. **Choose your field and topic.** Paid positions are often tied to a specific project.
2. **Search official sources.** University job pages, research institute websites and doctoral school calls.
3. **Contact potential supervisors early.** Our guide on [how to email a professor](/guides/how-to-email-a-professor-for-a-phd-scholarship/) has a template.
4. **Prepare strong documents.** A clear [research proposal](/guides/how-to-write-a-research-proposal-for-a-scholarship/), an academic [CV](/guides/how-to-write-a-cv-for-a-scholarship/) and good [recommendation letters](/guides/recommendation-letters/).
5. **Check language rules.** Many programmes only ask for documents in English, while others name a test score.
