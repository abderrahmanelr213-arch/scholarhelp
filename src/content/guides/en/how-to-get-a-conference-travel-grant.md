## Short answer

**A conference travel grant pays some or all of your costs to attend a conference, usually registration plus a fixed amount for travel and hotel.** They are offered by conferences, scientific societies, foundations and universities, often with special places for students and for people from low- and middle-income countries. Apply early, read exactly what is covered, and write a short statement that links the conference to your work.

## Who offers conference travel grants?

- **The conference itself.** Many large conferences have a "grants", "bursaries" or "scholarships" page, often with places for students or for participants from lower-income countries.
- **Scientific and professional societies.** Societies in medicine, computing, science and the humanities fund students to attend their own meetings and other conferences.
- **Foundations and international organisations.** Some fund people from specific regions or groups. For example, the **[UN Voluntary Fund for Indigenous Peoples](/opportunities/un-indigenous-peoples-fund-travel-grants-2027/)** pays travel for Indigenous representatives to attend UN sessions.
- **Your own university.** Many universities and departments have a small travel fund. Ask your supervisor or the graduate office.

## What does a travel grant usually cover?

It depends on the grant. It may include:

- **free or reduced registration**
- **a fixed amount for flight and hotel**, often higher if you travel from another continent
- **meals or a daily allowance** during the conference
- **free extra events**, such as workshops or mentoring sessions

Most grants are **partially funded**: you may still pay part of your flight, your visa or extra nights. Some pay only after the conference, when you send your receipts, so plan how you will cover the costs first.

## When should I apply?

Earlier than you think. Grant deadlines often close three to six months before the conference, well before normal registration ends. Follow the conferences in your field and note their grant dates as soon as the call is announced.

## How do I write a strong travel grant application?

1. **Explain why this conference.** Name the sessions, people or topics that connect to your study or work.
2. **Show what you will do with it.** Say how you will use what you learn, for example in your thesis, your hospital, your team or your community.
3. **Be honest about need.** If you have no other funding, say so in one clear sentence.
4. **Keep it short and specific.** One focused paragraph is better than a long, general essay. Our guide on [how to write a motivation letter](/guides/how-to-write-a-motivation-letter/) helps.
5. **Ask for any support letter early.** Some grants need a letter from your supervisor before the deadline. See our tips on [recommendation letters](/guides/recommendation-letters/).

## How do I avoid paid "conferences" that look funded?

A real academic or professional conference is run by a known society, university or organisation. Be careful with events that charge an application fee, sell "self-funded" places next to a few "fully funded" ones, or name no real organiser. Read our guide to [fake scholarships and paid summits](/guides/fake-scholarships-and-paid-summits/). For youth events, see [how to get into a fully funded youth conference](/guides/how-to-get-into-fully-funded-youth-conferences/).
