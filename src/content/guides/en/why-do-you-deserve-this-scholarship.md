## Short answer

**You deserve a scholarship when you can show three things: what you have already done, what you will do with the award, and how that matches what the funder wants to achieve.** Open with one clear sentence about your goal, give one or two real examples with results, explain how this scholarship is the next step, and end with the impact you will have. Keep it specific, honest and short.

## What is the committee really asking?

They are not asking you to praise yourself. They want to know:

- **Will this person use the money well?** Your record shows this.
- **Does their goal match our mission?** Read the funder's page and use its words, such as leadership, development, research or community.
- **Why this person and not another strong applicant?** One specific story makes you memorable.

## How do I structure my answer?

Use four short parts:

1. **Your goal.** One sentence: what you want to do and why it matters.
2. **Your proof.** One or two achievements with a result, for example a project, a job, volunteering or a top grade.
3. **The fit.** Why this scholarship and this programme are the right next step, naming something it offers.
4. **Your impact.** What will change for your community, field or country after you finish.

## What does a good answer look like?

**Weak:** "I deserve this scholarship because I am hardworking, passionate and I come from a poor family."

**Stronger:** "I want to improve mother and child care in rural clinics in my province. As a nursing student, I started a weekly health talk for pregnant women that grew from 8 to 60 women in one year. A master's in public health would give me the research skills to measure what works and share it with the health ministry. With this scholarship I can study full time, and I will return to lead a clinic training programme."

The second answer has a clear goal, a real result, a link to the programme and a plan to give back.

## Should I mention financial need?

If the scholarship is need-based, yes. Be honest and brief: one or two sentences about your situation, then return to your goals. Need explains why the money matters. Your plans explain why you deserve it.

## What mistakes should I avoid?

- **No proof:** every claim needs an example.
- **Too much life story:** keep background to a few lines that connect to your goal.
- **Ignoring the funder:** an answer that fits any scholarship fits none.
- **Copying samples:** committees recognise copied answers. Use your own words.
- **Breaking the word limit:** it shows you did not read the instructions.
