## Short answer

**Yes, you can win funding with a low GPA, if you apply to the right opportunities and make the rest of your application strong.** First check the minimum grade on each official page and skip the ones you do not meet. Then focus on programmes that value experience and motivation, such as youth summits, leadership and exchange programmes, and travel grants. Finally, let your experience, letters and plan show who you are now.

## Which scholarships care less about grades?

Grades matter most for top research degrees and merit scholarships at famous universities. They often matter less for:

- **youth conferences and summits**, which look at leadership, community work and ideas. See [how to get into fully funded youth conferences](/guides/how-to-get-into-fully-funded-youth-conferences/).
- **exchange and leadership programmes**, which look at motivation and impact
- **conference travel grants**, which look at your work or research. See [how to get a conference travel grant](/guides/how-to-get-a-conference-travel-grant/).
- **some government scholarships**, which may set only a pass mark and judge the rest on your plan and interview

Always read the eligibility section. If a programme says "minimum GPA 3.5", it usually means it.

## Should you explain a low GPA?

Only if there is a real reason and only in a few words. For example: "I worked 20 hours a week during my first two years. My grades rose from 2.6 to 3.4 once I could focus on my studies." This shows honesty and growth. Do not blame teachers, and do not spend a whole paragraph on it. Our guide on [how to write a motivation letter](/guides/how-to-write-a-motivation-letter/) shows where such a line fits.

## How do you make the rest of your application stronger?

- **Show an upward trend.** Point out better grades in later years or in the subjects closest to your field.
- **Get strong letters.** A supervisor who knows your work well can speak about skills that grades miss. See [recommendation letters](/guides/recommendation-letters/).
- **Show real experience.** Jobs, volunteering, projects, research or a small business all count.
- **Write a clear plan.** Say what you will study, why, and what you will do afterwards. See [how to write a study plan](/guides/how-to-write-a-study-plan-for-a-scholarship/).
- **Get the language part right.** A good English score removes one more doubt. If tests are hard to afford, look at [funded opportunities without IELTS](/guides/funded-opportunities-without-ielts/).

## What should you avoid?

- Applying everywhere without checking the minimum grade. It wastes time and fees.
- Paying anyone who promises a scholarship "regardless of grades". Read our warning on [fake scholarships and paid summits](/guides/fake-scholarships-and-paid-summits/).
- Hiding or changing your grades. Universities check transcripts, and false documents can get you banned.
