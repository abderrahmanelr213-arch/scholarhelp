## Short answer

**Almost every scholarship asks for the same core documents: passport, transcripts and degree, CV, motivation letter, recommendation letters and, often, an English test.** Prepare them once, keep clean PDF copies in one folder, and adapt only the motivation letter for each programme. Start six weeks before the deadline, because letters, translations and test results take the longest.

## What is on the standard checklist?

1. **Passport copy.** The photo page, valid for the whole programme. If yours expires soon, renew it now; some visas need six months of validity.
2. **Degree certificate and transcripts.** Your latest degree and the marks for every year. Final-year students usually send a current transcript.
3. **Translations.** Official translations if your documents are not in the programme's language.
4. **CV.** One or two pages, newest first: education, experience, projects, skills, languages. Some European programmes ask for the Europass format.
5. **Motivation letter or personal statement.** Why this programme, what you have done, and what you will do after. Read our guide on [how to write a motivation letter](/guides/how-to-write-a-motivation-letter/).
6. **Recommendation letters.** Usually one to three, from teachers or supervisors who know your work. See our guide to [recommendation letters](/guides/recommendation-letters/).
7. **English test result.** IELTS, TOEFL or similar, unless the programme accepts a degree taught in English or names no test. Every ScholarHelp listing states the exact language rule.
8. **Research proposal.** For PhDs, research fellowships and some master's by research. Our guide to [writing a research proposal](/guides/how-to-write-a-research-proposal-for-a-scholarship/) has a simple template.

Some programmes also ask for a portfolio, writing sample, video, proof of work experience or a medical form. These are always listed on the official page.

## Which documents take the longest?

- **Recommendation letters:** referees are busy. Ask at least three weeks before the deadline and send them a short reminder a week before.
- **English tests:** booking plus results can take two to four weeks.
- **Translations and certified copies:** allow one to two weeks.
- **Transcripts from your university:** some offices take days or weeks to issue sealed or stamped copies.

## How do I prepare my files so they are accepted?

- Save each document as a separate PDF unless the portal asks for one combined file.
- Name files clearly: your name plus the document type.
- Keep each file under the size limit; compress large scans.
- Check that scans are complete, readable and in colour where stamps matter.
- Make sure your name is spelled the same way on every document. If it differs (for example in transliteration), add a short note or an official name document.

## What mistakes get applications rejected early?

- Missing one required document, even a small one.
- Uploading the wrong file in a slot, such as a CV where the transcript should be.
- Sending a generic motivation letter that names another university.
- A recommendation letter that arrives after the deadline.
- Applying when you do not meet the minimum requirements, such as the degree level or test score.
