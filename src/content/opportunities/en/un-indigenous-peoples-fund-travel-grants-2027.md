## About

The United Nations Voluntary Fund for Indigenous Peoples helps representatives of Indigenous peoples and organisations take part in UN meetings about their rights. It is managed by the UN Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR).

The 2027 call funds participation in two UN sessions. It is a real chance to speak for your community at the UN, meet other Indigenous leaders and learn how UN human rights bodies work. Amazigh activists from North Africa, for example, can apply if they meet the criteria.

## Host institution / country

- **26th session of the UN Permanent Forum on Indigenous Issues:** 10 to 21 May 2027, UN Headquarters, New York, United States
- **20th session of the Expert Mechanism on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples:** 12 to 16 July 2027, Geneva, Switzerland

## Financial benefits

Selected participants receive a travel arrangement and a stipend to cover travel, accommodation and related costs, following UN rules.

## Eligibility criteria

- Indigenous individuals and representatives of Indigenous peoples' organisations
- Actively working to promote and protect the rights of Indigenous peoples
- Able to show how attending will help advocacy for their community or organisation
- In need of financial support to attend
- Indigenous persons with disabilities and Indigenous 2SLGBTQ+ persons are especially encouraged
- **Language requirement:** no language test. The application form is available in English, French and Spanish.

## Required documents

- A recent CV
- A copy of your passport
- A recommendation letter from the Indigenous organisation, community, NGO or non-profit you represent (no fixed template)

## How to apply

1. Read the official call for applications.
2. Prepare your CV, passport copy and recommendation letter.
3. Fill in the online application form in English, French or Spanish (links are on the official call page).
4. Submit before **8 November 2026, 23:59 Geneva time**. Late, incomplete or emailed applications are not accepted.

## Selection process and timeline

Applications are judged on five points:

1. **Representation:** a clear mandate from your community or organisation
2. **Relevance:** your work on Indigenous human rights, including with the UN body you want to attend
3. **Impact:** how your participation can strengthen advocacy at national, regional or international level
4. **Experience:** past advocacy, consultations, submissions or community work
5. **Need:** financial need and no other funding

Only selected candidates are contacted. The Fund does not answer individual questions about results.

## Tips for a strong application

- Ask your organisation for a recommendation letter that clearly says you represent them.
- Name the specific issues you will raise at the session.
- Mention earlier advocacy work, even at local level.
- Submit early: the online form does not accept late applications.
