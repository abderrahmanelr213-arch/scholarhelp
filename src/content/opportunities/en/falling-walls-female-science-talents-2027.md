## About

The Female Science Talents Intensive Track is a one-year career programme of the Falling Walls Foundation in Berlin. It selects 20 early-career women scientists from all disciplines and helps them build an international network, get visibility for their research and grow as leaders.

The programme runs from February 2027 to January 2028. It mixes online sessions with two in-person gatherings in Berlin, held during the Falling Walls Science Summit, one of the best-known science events in Europe.

## Host institution / country

The Falling Walls Foundation is a non-profit based in Berlin, Germany. Its yearly Science Summit brings together researchers, entrepreneurs and leaders from around the world.

## What the programme offers

- **Two gatherings in Berlin:** a spring gathering in May (4 days) and a fall gathering in November, during the Falling Walls Science Summit 2027
- **Workshops and career talks:** science entrepreneurship, science communication, leadership and pitch training
- **Mentoring** from programme alumni
- **Networking** with women leaders and the Falling Walls communities
- **Visibility** for your work on Falling Walls channels
- **Certificate** of participation

## Financial benefits

- Travel to Berlin: flights from outside Europe are booked by the Foundation; trains and other travel are reimbursed against receipts
- Accommodation and meals during the gatherings
- Invitation letter and documents for your visa

Not covered: visa fees, taxis and travel insurance. There is no stipend. The programme is free of charge.

## Eligibility criteria

- Women (cis and trans), genderqueer and non-binary researchers
- PhD students in the final stage of their PhD, or postdocs. The official page says the PhD should be finished in 2021 or later (the FAQ says 2020 or later). Equivalent degrees, such as medical degrees, are welcome
- Any discipline, from institutions worldwide. Women from the Global South are especially encouraged
- Able to join about one or two online sessions a month and at least 80% of the programme
- Willing to mentor the next cohort
- **Language requirement:** fluent English. No test certificate is asked.

## Required documents

- Answers to five short questions (300 characters each): about you, your research, your career goals, how the programme helps you and what you can bring
- CV in table format
- A video introduction of up to 2 minutes (MP4)

## How to apply

1. Read the call on the official page.
2. Prepare your CV, short answers and video.
3. Submit on the official application portal before **15 November 2026, 23:59 CET**.

## Selection process and timeline

- A jury selects participants based on scientific excellence, motivation, potential and self-presentation.
- Decisions are made by the end of December 2026, and selected participants are emailed in January 2027.
- The programme starts with a kick-off session in January or February 2027.

## Tips for a strong application

- Keep each answer short and concrete: you only have 300 characters.
- In the video, explain your research so a non-specialist understands why it matters.
- Show leadership or outreach you already do, such as teaching, mentoring or science communication.
- Start your visa process as soon as you are selected: it can take 8 to 12 weeks.
